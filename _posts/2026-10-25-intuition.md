## 1. Limitations of AI-driven autonomous discovery
Although recent AI-driven autonomous discovery seem promising. There are some patterns and limitations observed by math researchers.

In Terence Tao's blog, Jeremy Avigad writes: "the AI-generated solutions to open problems we have seen all have a similar character: they are problems that AI could solve by cobbling together available techniques." (The Future of Mathematics)[https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2026/10/05/the-future-of-mathematics/#:~:text=the%20AI%2Dgenerated%20solutions%20to%20open%20problems%20we%20have%20seen%20all%20have%20a%20similar%20character%3A%20they%20are%20problems%20that%20AI%20could%20solve%20by%20cobbling%20together%20available%20techniques.]

More specifically, in the Navier-Stokes solution by OpenAI, the ingenious convex integration solution stemmted from ideas in existing literature [https://x.com/scottnarmstrong/status/2114412308185100522]. The solution to OpenAI's unit distance problem was connecting planar discrete geometry to algebraic number theory, using class field towers and Golod–Shafarevich theory.

Even earlier, several researchers have pointed out AI's first IMO gold medal and especially its strong performance in geometry problems were utilizing AI's capability in mass trial and error essentially turning the problem into a combinatorics setting.

Now I want to distinguish proof and impact. Proof is mostly highly constrained in its problem setup. But even before proving if something is true or has certain properties, one can induce those from intuition, witout knowing full proof, and leverage that conclusion to build on it, working on new projects.

First, I want to distinguish proofs and impact. I found Mingyang Deng's comments to be notable how machine learning is an empirical field that is based on intuition. Even if you are unable to completely understand the underlying mechanism or prove a method, the objective of the field is to get results that are useful and impactful. 

Not knowing full proofs is not new, as Tiserman stated he doesn't know all the details in the proofs he used in his own proofs that lead to his Fields medal.

While problems that require causal reasoning and future forecasting seem to lack greatly. Especially with retrieval of existing and current works, I've noticed AI is very disinclined to accept future predictions that deviate from status quo, likely enhanced by post-training in verifiable rewards. For instance, whether the US would attack Iran? Asked weeks earlier, is heavily inclined to deny it. And unwilling to accept truths that are not officially verified such as Jeffrey Epstein's connections to the IDF. Also the more abstract, broader the given objective, LLMs are poor at pruning the search tree and making progress in a sample-efficient manner. 

For example, OpenAI gained great publicity, views, and mentions from publishing the math results. But would the LLM have chosen that direction if it were prompted to do something that gets us this many views, mentions? And willing to use tens of millions of dollars in the process and think it is worth it? Abstract problems are harder, especially for problems that don't have verifiable solutions, in that example above, the LLM would concoct several potential projects to work on. Each with a trade-off in terms of risk-reward, timeline/cost to succeed, and reputation effects. 

These are where humans will excel even in the era of superintelligence. 

As karpathy said, LLMs are a lossely compression algorithm. Pre-training inherently has mode-seeking tendencies and regressing to the average of the data distribution. Post-training enhances mode-seeking behavior. Explicit, verifiable reward guided training succumb to prioritizing existing facts and truths. 

This leads to some failure modes I've observed personally. On top of training incentivizing mode-seeking, retrieval based responses severely handicap the model's ability to model the future and have accurate predictive capabilities by having bias towards the current status and current state staying as is.

Benchmark studies support the frontier models' lack of predictive capabilities. This is apparent as LLMs aren't trained to understand the concept of progression of time and temporal differences and developments. Whereas we, as humans have a predictive model of the world that we live and embody everyday and update our priors very frequently, several times a day as we encounter out of distribution events.

## 2. What can humans do better and levereage this capability best?
There are some examples of abstractions and human intuition based refinement.

Most recent notable example of utilizing human intuition and AI-driven proof is the (KLS Conjecture)[https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/blog/solving-a-31-year-old-grand-challenge-in-mathematics-the-kls-conjecture]. AI agents explored roughly 100 mathematical directions, many involving higher-order tensor calculations. Researchers examined intermediate results and eliminated unpromising approaches. The final proof used recursive refinement, with a decisive idea inspired by the researchers' earlier work on fast linear-programming algorithms.

But wouldn't AI be capable enough to not need intermediate intervention by humans and prune the search tree themselves? Yes, but in problems where the solution is verifiable first. The examples given my AI-driven problem solving has been constrained to math. But AI will be used to solve more real world problems and the preference, objective, budget, and trade-offs will have to be much more well aligned personally and a lot of cases the success can't be verified explicitly and only defined by the user. 

Therefore human-aligned AI will not just be useful for safety purposes but for capabilites and decision making, problem solving regarding real world tasks and problems in one's personal life and in business. In the context of organizations and businesses, the decision maker has different appetite, priorities, risk-averseness, time-horizons for their objectives, an AI could present two business plans where each has different risk-reward trade-offs and time-horizon to see success, budgets. There is no objective truth to this search process, only very personal preferences. 

So personal decisions to business decisions, political decisions are very human and has no objective truth. The alignment to human intuition can only come from if it trains while being in people's shoes from brith to present.

## 3. Next training paradigm to advance predictive reasoning.
To solve these problems there is a new way to train LLMs. Interacting with the real-world continuos real-time predictions, action selections, and update based on these roll-outs in the real-world.

Then how do you do this practicallly in the real-world? The most likely way this will transpire is through a personal device like smart glasses with heads-up display to take in every input the person wearing it is wearing and personalized LLMs giving recommendations on which actions to take in real-time based on their objectives. Deployed in the scale of millions, these continuous roll-outs at scale in the real-world with the training signal based on predictions on the future by optimizing current action will provide sufficient training signal to induce a different axis in reasoning. 

Getting humans to be aligned with and follow actions recommended by AI would be a separate axis that needs to be addressed, but the human brain is by default lazy and will naturally rely on extenral computations, like how we reply on feeds to see what to watch, read, and do; it seems natural to be more reliant on AI for more portions of our decisions (for most people).
